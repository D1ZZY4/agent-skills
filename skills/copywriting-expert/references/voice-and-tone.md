# Voice and Tone

Voice and tone defaults for product copy. Project-specific guidance and audience needs take precedence.

## Core principles

- **Clear over clever.** Personality should never make users decode a functional message.
- **Plain language over unnecessary jargon.** Use domain terms when they are correct and useful for the audience.
- **Direct address when appropriate.** Use `you` when it matches the product's register and locale. Do not force direct address into formal or regulated copy.
- **Describe outcomes users can understand.** Prefer `Your changes are saved` over an internal implementation description.
- **Be specific.** Concrete facts are more useful than generic reassurance.

## Tone follows stakes

| Stakes | Default tone |
| --- | --- |
| Low | Warm, concise, personality allowed |
| Medium | Neutral, helpful, direct |
| High | Precise, serious, explicit, no cleverness |

Brand personality does not override safety, clarity, or consequence-aware wording.

## Confidence and claims

A confident voice is not permission to overclaim.

Avoid unverified guarantees such as:

- `always`
- `never`
- `instant`
- `fully secure`
- `completely private`
- `no risk`

Use absolute language only when product behavior and project guidance support it.

## Sentence case

Use sentence case by default for UI text when the project does not document another convention.

Do not rewrite existing copy solely to enforce sentence case unless a broader consistency pass is authorized.

## Domain and audience

Learn the project's vocabulary before choosing register.

- Expert audiences often need precise domain terms, not simplified substitutes.
- Broad audiences may need a technical term defined on first use.
- Regulated or safety-critical audiences need exact terminology where the domain requires it.
- Localization can make a direct English phrase unnatural. Follow the target locale's conventions.

## Anti-AI-sounding patterns

Avoid filler that makes copy sound generated rather than authored:

- `delve into`
- `leverage`
- `utilize`
- `robust`
- `seamless`
- `cutting-edge`
- `revolutionary`
- `game-changer`
- `empower`
- `It's important to note that`
- `In today's fast-paced world`
- `Furthermore` / `Moreover` used as decorative signposts

Replace with direct facts or remove the sentence entirely.

Examples:

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| `Utilize our robust search to seamlessly optimize your workflow.` | `Search filters narrow results as you type.` |
| `It's important to note that this action cannot be undone.` | `You can't undo this.` |
| `We leverage data to empower teams.` | `Reports update every 5 minutes.` |

The better example is valid only when the stated behavior is actually true.

## Consistency without monotony

Use consistent terminology and register, but do not force identical sentence structures onto every surface. Errors, empty states, onboarding, and destructive warnings have different jobs.

## Preserve product identity

Do not flatten a deliberately distinctive voice into generic corporate language. The goal is accurate, recognizable product communication with the minimum wording needed for the task.
